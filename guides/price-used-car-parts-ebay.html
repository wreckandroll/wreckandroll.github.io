How to price used car parts on eBay, using real sold prices

Price a used part too high and it sits on the shelf for months. Price it too low and you give money away. The fix is simple: price from what buyers have actually paid for the same part, not from what other sellers are asking.

Asking prices vs sold prices

Live listings only show what sellers hope to get. Some of those parts have been sitting unsold for a year. Sold prices show what buyers really paid, so they are the number to price from.

There are two free ways to see them:

  • The Sold items filter. Search eBay for the part, then tick Sold items in the filters. Quick, and anyone can use it.
  • Product Research in Seller Hub. eBay’s own research tool (it used to be called Terapeak). It is free to all sellers under the Research tab in Seller Hub, and shows the average sold price, the sold price range, postage costs and a price trend.

Product Research is the better of the two because its sold prices include accepted Best Offers. The normal Sold filter shows the asking price, even when the buyer paid less. To use Product Research you need to have made at least one sale and opted in to Seller Hub.

Step 1: search for the exact part

A general search such as “Corolla mirror” mixes in different years, sides and versions, and the average becomes meaningless. Search as narrowly as you can:

  • OEM part number first. If you have it, search by it. This is the most accurate comparison you can get. See how to find the OEM part number by VIN.
  • Otherwise make, model, years and part, plus the side and the version (power fold or manual, LED or halogen, with or without camera).
  • Filter to Used condition and to your own country, because postage and demand differ between markets.

Step 2: look at the right numbers

What to checkWhy it matters
Sold price rangeIgnore the very highest and lowest sales. They are usually a different version, a damaged part or a lucky sale.
Middle (typical) priceThis is your starting point.
How many soldPlenty of sales means steady demand. Only one or two means the price is less reliable.
Live competing listingsIf twenty sellers have the same part, buyers have choice. Price near the middle or a little below.
PostageCheck whether the sold prices included free postage. A bumper with free postage and one with paid postage are not the same price.

Step 3: adjust for your part

  • Condition. Scratches, broken tabs or a cracked housing lower the price. Price below the typical sale and show the damage clearly in the photos and description.
  • Completeness. A mirror with its glass, cover and indicator is worth more than a bare unit. A head unit with its trim and wiring plug is worth more than the screen alone.
  • Tested or not. A part you have tested and can say works is worth more than an untested one.
  • Size and postage. For large, heavy parts, postage can cost more than the part. Work out your real cost before you offer free postage.
  • eBay fees. Your price has to cover the fees and still leave a margin. See how eBay fees on car parts work and how returns raise them.

Step 4: decide how fast you want it gone

Not every part should be priced the same way:

  • Fast-moving parts (common models, parts that sell every week): price at or slightly below the typical sold price and let volume do the work.
  • Rare parts (low-volume models, hard-to-find versions): price at the top of the range. Few sellers have them, and the right buyer will pay.
  • Bulky parts taking up space in the yard: a lower price can be worth it to free the shelf.

Turning on Best Offer helps on slower parts. Buyers make an offer instead of leaving, and you still decide whether to accept.

Step 5: review prices that are not selling

Check unsold listings every month or two. If a part has had plenty of views but no sale, check the sold prices again. The market may have dropped, or a cheaper seller may have appeared. If it has had very few views, the problem is usually the title or item specifics, not the price. See how to write eBay titles for used car parts.

Common mistakes

  • Pricing from asking prices instead of sold prices
  • Comparing with a different version, side or year range
  • Copying a price that included free postage, then charging postage on top
  • Pricing every part in the yard with the same rule
  • Never reviewing old listings

Frequently asked questions

Is eBay Product Research free?

Yes. It is free to all sellers under the Research tab in Seller Hub. You need at least one sale to opt in to Seller Hub.

How do I see what a car part sold for on eBay?

Search for the part, then tick Sold items in the filters. For more detail, including accepted Best Offer prices, use Product Research in Seller Hub.

Should I price used parts below new aftermarket parts?

Usually, yes, unless the part is genuine and the aftermarket version is poor quality or not available. Buyers compare both, so check the new aftermarket price as well.

Quick checklist

  • Searched by OEM number, or by exact make, model, years, side and version
  • Used sold prices, not asking prices
  • Ignored the highest and lowest sales
  • Adjusted for condition, completeness and testing
  • Checked postage and eBay fees
  • Set a date to review it if it does not sell
