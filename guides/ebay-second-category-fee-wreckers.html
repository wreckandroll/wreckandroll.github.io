eBay second category fee: the hidden monthly cost for wreckers

Many yards list parts in two eBay categories without knowing it costs extra. On a few listings it is small change. Across hundreds of Good ’Til Cancelled listings it can add up to hundreds of dollars a month, for almost no extra sales.

What eBay charges

On eBay Australia, listing an item in a second category is a paid listing upgrade:

  • AUD 1.00 (incl. GST) per listing for the second category, even when the listing itself is free.
  • Other upgrades are charged twice. If the listing has a subtitle or another paid upgrade, you pay it once for each category.
  • The higher final value fee applies. If the two categories have different final value fees, eBay charges the higher one when the part sells.

Check eBay’s current fee page for your account, as fees change from time to time.

Why it becomes a monthly cost

Most used parts are listed as fixed price, Good ’Til Cancelled. These listings renew automatically every month until the part sells, and eBay can charge listing upgrade fees again at each renewal. A slow-selling part can carry the second-category fee month after month.

How it happens

Yard systems that list straight to eBay, such as Pinnacle Professional, can send a second eBay category along with the main one. If a second category has been set up or selected, every part listed that way carries the fee, and nobody notices until they read the invoice line by line.

eBay category vs eBay Store category

These are easy to mix up:

  • eBay categories are eBay’s own marketplace categories. A second one costs extra.
  • eBay Store categories are the sections of your own eBay Store page. If you have a Store, you can use a main and a secondary Store category for free.

Only the second eBay category is charged.

Is a second category worth it?

For used car parts, usually not. If the part sits in the right, specific category with compatibility added, buyers who search by their vehicle or by part number already find it. A second category rarely adds sales for a part like a door mirror or a head unit. It is better to spend the effort on the right main category, a clear title and complete item specifics. See choosing the right eBay category for used car parts.

How to check if you are paying it

  1. Open your eBay invoice or fee details in Seller Hub.
  2. Look for fees for listing in a second category.
  3. Count how many listings carry it and multiply by the fee. That is your monthly cost.
  4. Open a few live listings and check whether a second category is set.

How to stop it

  1. Stop new ones. In your yard system, select only one eBay category when listing. If your system has a default eBay template or setting that adds a second category, remove it. Ask your software provider if you cannot find it.
  2. Clean up live listings. Remove the second category from active listings. Seller Hub’s bulk edit helps when many listings are affected.
  3. Keep the right main category. Make sure the one category you keep is specific and allows compatibility.
  4. Check the next invoice to confirm the fee has gone.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a second category cost on eBay Australia?

AUD 1.00 including GST per listing, and any other paid upgrades on the listing are charged twice.

Is a second eBay Store category charged?

No. If you have an eBay Store, a main and a secondary Store category are free. Only a second eBay marketplace category is charged.

Will removing the second category hurt my sales?

For most used car parts, no, as long as the main category is correct and has compatibility.

Quick checklist

  • Invoice checked for second-category fees
  • Second category removed from the yard system’s listing settings
  • Second category removed from live listings
  • One correct, specific category with compatibility kept
  • Next invoice checked
