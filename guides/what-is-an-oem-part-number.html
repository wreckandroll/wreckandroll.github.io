What is an OEM part number? Original, OEM and aftermarket numbers explained

Quick answer: an OEM part number is the car maker’s own number for a part. It is also called the original, genuine or factory part number. It is the most reliable way to identify the exact part a car needs.

Names that mean the same thing

You will see several names for the car maker’s number. In practice they usually mean the same:

  • OEM number (original equipment manufacturer)
  • OE number (original equipment)
  • Original part number
  • Genuine part number
  • Factory part number

Other numbers you will see on parts and listings

NumberWhat it isSame as OEM?
Supplier numberThe number used by the company that made the part for the car maker, often on labels of electrical parts and lightsNo, but it can help confirm the part
Aftermarket numberThe number of a replacement part made by another companyNo
Interchange or cross-referenceA list of numbers or vehicles a part also fitsNo, it links numbers together
Casting numberA number cast or stamped into a metal part during manufactureNot usually
MPN on eBayThe Manufacturer Part Number field in a listingFor a genuine part, use the OEM number here

What OEM part numbers look like

Each car maker has its own format. Two real examples from our guides:

  • Toyota: 87909-06451. A left mirror sub-assembly for a Camry. The first five digits identify the type of part, the last five the version.
  • Volkswagen: 5G2 857 507 BN. A left exterior mirror housing for a Golf. Letters at the end often mark a version, and a colour code can follow, such as 9B9 for satin black.

Small differences matter. Two numbers that differ by one character are often different parts, for example left and right.

Superseded numbers

Car makers sometimes replace a part number with a newer one for the same part. The old number is “superseded”. Buyers may search by either, so it helps to record both.

How to find the OEM number for your part

  1. Look it up by VIN in an online parts catalogue. This is the most reliable way. Step by step: how to find OEM part numbers by VIN.
  2. Read it from the part, if there is a label or stamp. See where to find the part number on a car part.
  3. Check it matches: side, production date range and version.

Frequently asked questions

What does OEM part number mean?

It is the number the car maker gives a part. OEM stands for original equipment manufacturer.

Is an OEM number the same as the original part number?

Yes. OEM number, original part number, genuine part number and factory part number usually mean the same thing.

Is the OEM number the same as the MPN on eBay?

For a genuine used part, yes. Put the OEM number in both Manufacturer Part Number and Reference OE/OEM Number.

What is a superseded part number?

A newer number that replaces an older one for the same part. Both can be useful to buyers, so record both if you know them.
