Where to find the part number on a car part

Quick answer: look for a label, sticker or stamp on the part itself. Lights and electrical parts usually have a label; metal parts are often stamped. If you can’t find one, look the number up by the car’s VIN, which is often the more reliable way anyway.

Where to look, by type of part

PartWhere to look
Headlights and tail lightsA label on the back or underside of the housing
Door mirrorsA label on the base or inside the housing; the glass and cover often have their own numbers
Head units, ECUs and other modulesA sticker on the casing
Alternators and starter motorsA label or plate on the body, often with a supplier’s number as well
Engines and transmissionsA stamped engine or transmission code. This identifies the unit, but is usually not a part number
Body panels (bonnet, doors, bumpers)Often no readable number. Use a VIN lookup
WheelsMarkings cast on the inside of the rim or spokes
Seatbelts and airbag partsA label on the belt, buckle or module

Exact positions vary by make and model, so check the whole part, including the underside.

Numbers that are not the OEM number

  • Supplier numbers: the maker of the part for the car company, such as a lighting or electrical supplier, often prints its own number too.
  • Casting numbers: numbers formed into metal parts during manufacture.
  • Date codes and barcodes: production dates and internal tracking.

If a label shows several numbers, compare them with a VIN lookup to find which is the car maker’s number. More on the differences: what is an OEM part number.

When there is no number: use the VIN

The VIN tells a parts catalogue exactly which car the part came from. You then find the part on a diagram and read its number, including the right side and version. Step by step with screenshots: how to find OEM part numbers by VIN.

Tips

  • Take a clear photo of the label before cleaning or handling the part further.
  • Clean gently. Solvents can wipe printed labels away.
  • Check the side. Left and right parts usually have different numbers. See driver side vs passenger side.
  • Write the number down everywhere it is needed: your records, the part tag and the listing.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I find my OEM part number?

Check the part for a label, sticker or stamp. If there is none, or it is unreadable, look the number up by the car’s VIN in an online parts catalogue.

Why can’t I find a number on my part?

Many parts, such as body panels, have no readable number, or the label has worn off. Use a VIN lookup instead.

Is the number on the label always the right one to order?

Not always. Labels can show a supplier’s number or a date code instead of the car maker’s number. Confirm it with a VIN lookup.
