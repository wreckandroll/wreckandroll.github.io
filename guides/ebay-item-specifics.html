How to fill eBay item specifics for used car parts

A practical guide for wreckers and used parts sellers. Item specifics are the details buyers filter by, so filling them properly helps the right buyer find your part.

What item specifics are

When you list a part, eBay asks for details such as brand, part number, placement and colour. eBay splits them into Required and Additional (optional). Some optional ones show how many buyers search for them, for example colour or number of pieces. Optional does not mean unimportant: buyers use these details to narrow their search.

Step 1: start with a strong title

Write the title first, with make, model, side, generation, key features and years. eBay’s List faster box can then pull some specifics from your title, such as brand and placement. Tick them, but check each one is correct.

Use the Custom label (SKU) for your stock and tag numbers, so the part is easy to find in the yard when it sells.

Step 2: fill every required specific

Required specifics, such as brand, must be filled before the listing can go live. Pick the value from eBay’s list where possible rather than typing your own, so it matches what buyers filter by.

Step 3: the optional specifics that matter most for car parts

SpecificWhat to enter
Manufacturer Part NumberThe OEM number you found from the VIN. See how to find OEM part numbers.
Reference OE/OEM NumberThe same OEM number, so buyers searching either field find it.
Placement on VehicleLeft or right, front or rear. Check it against the actual part. In right-hand-drive countries, left is the passenger side.
ColourThe colour of the part itself, not just the car.
TypeWhether it is a full assembly or a single piece.
FeaturesOnly features the part really has, such as heated, power fold or indicator.
Part-specific fieldsFor example Mirror Adjustment Method for mirrors. Fill them from the part, not from memory.
Number of PiecesUsually 1 for a single used part.

Step 4: add compatibility separately

Fitment is not an item specific. Add it in the compatibility section, matched to the donor vehicle’s engine and body, so buyers who search by their car see the part.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the part number blank. Many buyers and mechanics search by number.
  • Wrong side. Always confirm left or right against the part, not the photo angle.
  • Guessing features. A wrong feature leads to returns. If you can’t confirm it, leave it out.
  • Copying another listing’s specifics without checking they match this part.

Quick checklist

  • Title with make, model, side, features and years
  • Stock and tag numbers in the SKU
  • All required specifics filled
  • OEM number in both part number fields
  • Placement, colour, type and features checked against the part
  • Compatibility added and matched to the donor vehicle
