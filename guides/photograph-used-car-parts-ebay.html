How to photograph used car parts for eBay: a shot list for wreckers

Buyers of used parts cannot hold the part. The photos have to answer their questions instead: is it the right part, which side, which version, and what condition. Good photos sell parts faster and stop many returns before they happen.

How many photos?

eBay lets you add up to 24 photos to a listing for free. Most used parts need between 6 and 12. Use more for expensive or complex parts such as engines, gearboxes and head units.

Two eBay rules to know:

  • No stock photos for used items. Show the actual part you are selling.
  • No text or logos on the photos. Information such as “free postage” or your business name goes in the title or description.

The shot list

ShotWhy buyers need it
1. Main photo: whole part, front viewShown in search results. It decides whether the buyer clicks.
2. Back viewShows mounting points, brackets and hidden damage.
3. Both sides or endsShows the shape, so the buyer can match it to their car.
4. Part number label or stamp, close upTrade buyers check this first. Make sure the number is readable.
5. Connectors and plugsPlug shape and pin count often show the version. Broken clips are a common return reason.
6. Mounting tabs, clips and bolt holesBroken tabs are easy to miss and a frequent “not as described” complaint.
7. Every mark or damage, close upShows the buyer exactly what they are getting. This protects you if they later claim it is not as described.
8. Working proof, if testedA head unit turned on, a light lit up, an odometer reading on a cluster.

Background and light

  • Plain background. A clean bench, a plain sheet or a painted wall. Not the yard floor with other parts in shot, which makes buyers unsure which part they are getting.
  • Daylight or bright, even light. Dark photos hide the part and look like you are hiding damage. Avoid strong shadows and flash glare on glass and chrome.
  • Clean the part. A quick wipe makes labels readable and damage clear. Do not hide damage.
  • Fill the frame. The part should take up most of the main photo.

Show the side clearly

Mirrors, headlights, tail lights, doors and guards come in left and right. Photograph the part so the side is obvious, for example the mirror from the front with its mounting side visible. Then write Left or Right in the title. See driver’s side left or right.

Photos for parts still on the car

Some yards list parts before removing them. If you do, take clear photos of the part on the car, plus a photo of the build plate or compliance plate where allowed, so buyers can see the model and year. Say clearly in the description that the part will be removed after purchase.

A quick routine for the yard

  1. Pull the part and wipe it down.
  2. Put it on the same plain background every time.
  3. Take the photos in the same order as the shot list above.
  4. Write the stock number on a tag and take one photo of it with the part, so the photos never get mixed up. Leave that photo out of the listing.
  5. Upload the photos into a folder named after the stock number.

A fixed routine means anyone in the yard can take the photos, and whoever lists the parts, in the yard or remotely, always has what they need. See how to inventory a wrecked vehicle part by part.

Frequently asked questions

How many photos can I add to an eBay listing?

Up to 24, free of charge.

Can I use a stock photo for a used car part?

No. eBay does not allow catalogue or stock photos for items listed as used. Show the actual part.

Which photo matters most?

The main photo, because it shows in search results. After that, a clear close-up of the part number label.

Quick checklist

  • Main photo: whole part, plain background, fills the frame
  • Back, sides and ends
  • Part number label readable
  • Connectors, tabs and mounting points
  • Every mark or damage, close up
  • Side (left or right) clear
  • No stock photos, no text on photos
