How to write eBay titles for used car parts, with examples

The title is the first thing eBay search reads and the first thing a buyer sees. A vague title such as “mirror for car” gets buried. A clear title with the right words in the right order gets found by the buyer who needs exactly that part.

The 80-character limit

eBay titles can be up to 80 characters, and spaces count. That is enough for the important details if you skip filler words. Try to use most of the space with useful words.

A simple formula

Most buyers search by car first, then part. Put the words in this order:

OrderWhat to writeExample
1Make and modelToyota Corolla
2Series or body code, if buyers use itZRE182, FK
3Year range the part fits2013-2018
4Side and positionLeft Front, Right Rear
5Part nameDoor Mirror
6Version detailsPower Fold, Indicator, LED, Heated
7Part number, if it fitsthe OEM number
8Genuine or Used, if space is leftGenuine

Examples

Weak titleBetter title
Mirror for carToyota Corolla 2013-2018 Left Door Mirror Power Fold Indicator Genuine Used
Honda radio screen good condition!!Honda Civic FK 2016-2021 Touchscreen Head Unit Radio Display Genuine
Outlander lightMitsubishi Outlander 2019 Right Headlight LED Genuine Used

These examples show the format. Always check the exact years, version and side against the actual part and the parts catalogue before you list.

Getting the side right

Write Left or Right in the title, judged from the driver’s seat facing forward. “Driver side” and “passenger side” mean different things in Australia and the USA, so they can confuse overseas buyers. If you have space, add it as well: “Left Passenger Side”. More on this in driver’s side left or right.

Part numbers in the title

Many professional buyers, such as mechanics and other wreckers, search by part number. If the OEM number fits within 80 characters, include it. If it does not, put it in the item specifics (Manufacturer Part Number and Reference OE/OEM Number), where eBay search still reads it. See what an OEM part number is and how to fill eBay item specifics.

Words to leave out

  • Filler: “great”, “L@@K”, “wow”, “bargain”, “must see”. Buyers do not search for them.
  • Repeated words and extra punctuation such as “!!!” or “***”.
  • Other brands or models it does not fit. Adding them to get more views breaks eBay rules and causes returns.
  • Postage or shop details such as “free post” or your business name. Those belong in the listing settings and description.
  • ALL CAPITALS. It is harder to read and looks like spam.

Title vs item specifics

The title and item specifics work together. The title gets the click. Item specifics are the filters buyers use, such as Placement on Vehicle, Brand and Manufacturer Part Number. Fill both. A great title with empty item specifics still misses buyers who filter.

Frequently asked questions

How long can an eBay title be?

Up to 80 characters, including spaces.

Should I put the part number in the eBay title?

Yes, if it fits. Many trade buyers search by part number. If it does not fit, put it in the item specifics.

Should I write driver side or left?

Write Left or Right first, judged from the driver’s seat. Add driver or passenger side after it if you have room.

Quick checklist

  • Make, model and years first
  • Left or right, front or rear
  • Clear part name buyers would search for
  • Version details that change fitment
  • Part number if it fits
  • No filler, no capitals, no other models it does not fit
  • Checked against the actual part
